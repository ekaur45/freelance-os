## Purpose

Realistic timelines protect both quality and reputation. Rushed, underestimated timelines lead to either burnout, cut corners, or blown deadlines — all of which damage trust more than a longer, honest estimate would.

---

# Timeline Estimation Principles

* Base timelines on the complexity tiers used in `EstimationRules.md`, not on what the client hopes to hear.
* Include time for testing, revisions, and deployment — not just feature-building.
* Account for dependency on the client (content, approvals, third-party access) separately from your own working time.
* Add buffer for genuinely new/unfamiliar technical territory (a new API, an unfamiliar framework, etc.).

# Milestone Planning

* Break any project longer than a few weeks into milestones with clear deliverables.
* Each milestone should represent something demonstrable, not just "time elapsed."
* Tie payments to milestones where possible (see `FixedPrice.md`).

---

# Communicating Timelines to Clients

* Give a range rather than a single hard date when uncertainty is genuine ("6-8 weeks" rather than a false-precision exact date).
* Flag dependencies on the client explicitly as part of the timeline ("assuming API credentials are provided within the first week").
* Never commit to a deadline under pressure without doing the estimation work first — see `Knowledge/08_Sales/ClientObjections.md` for handling "can you start today" pressure.

# Guiding Principle

A timeline should be a commitment you can actually keep under normal circumstances — not an optimistic best case presented as the expected outcome.
