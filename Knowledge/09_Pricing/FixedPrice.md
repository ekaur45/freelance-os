## Purpose

Fixed pricing gives clients budget certainty, but only works safely when scope is clearly defined. These guidelines keep fixed-price quotes profitable and honest.

---

# When Fixed Price Is Appropriate

* Requirements are clearly documented (spec, wireframes, or detailed job post).
* Scope is unlikely to change significantly during the project.
* The project is short-to-medium duration (see `TimelineGuidelines.md`).

Avoid fixed pricing when scope is vague — offer a paid discovery phase first instead (see `EstimationRules.md`).

---

# Converting Hours to Fixed Price

1. Estimate hours per component (see `EstimationRules.md`).
2. Multiply by hourly rate (`HourlyRates.md`).
3. Add risk buffer based on scope clarity and client history.
4. Round to a clean, presentable number.

---

# Handling Scope Changes on Fixed-Price Projects

* Anything outside the documented scope is billed as an addition (hourly, or a new fixed quote for the delta).
* Document the original scope clearly enough that "in scope vs. out of scope" is unambiguous later.
* See `Knowledge/08_Sales/Negotiation.md` for how to handle scope-creep conversations.

---

# Guiding Principle

A fixed price should be a number you'd stand behind even if the project takes slightly longer than expected — which means the buffer has to be real, not optimistic.
