# Meeting Scripts

## Purpose

These are structural guides for client calls — discovery calls, kickoff calls, and status/review calls — not word-for-word scripts to read verbatim. The goal is a natural conversation that consistently covers what needs to be covered.

---

# Before Any Call

* Review the job post, proposal, or previous messages so the client doesn't have to repeat context.
* Prepare 3-5 discovery questions relevant to the specific project (see `DiscoveryQuestions.md`).
* Have a rough idea of technical approach going in, without committing to specifics prematurely.

---

# Discovery Call Structure

## 1. Opening (2-3 minutes)

* Thank them for the time.
* Briefly confirm what you understand about the project so far.
* Set expectations for the call: "I'd like to understand the project better, ask a few questions, and see if there's a good fit — does that work?"

## 2. Understand the Business Problem (10-15 minutes)

* Ask what problem they're solving and for whom.
* Ask what success looks like.
* Let them talk more than you do at this stage.

## 3. Clarify Scope & Technical Details (10-15 minutes)

* Walk through must-have vs. nice-to-have features.
* Ask the relevant technical discovery questions.
* Note anything that significantly affects timeline or cost.

## 4. Share Initial Thoughts (5-10 minutes)

* Reflect back what you heard to confirm understanding.
* Share a high-level approach or relevant past experience — avoid over-committing to specifics before they're confirmed in writing.
* Be honest about anything uncertain; offer to follow up rather than guessing.

## 5. Next Steps (2-3 minutes)

* Confirm what happens next (a written proposal, an estimate, a follow-up call).
* Confirm timeline for that next step.
* Thank them again.

---

# Kickoff Call Structure (After Being Hired)

## 1. Confirm Scope & Success Criteria

* Restate the agreed scope in your own words and confirm alignment.
* Confirm what "done" looks like for the first milestone.

## 2. Confirm Logistics

* Communication channel and cadence (e.g., weekly async update, Slack, email).
* Access needed (repositories, environments, credentials, third-party accounts).
* Points of contact on both sides.

## 3. Confirm Timeline & Milestones

* Walk through milestones and expected dates.
* Flag any dependencies on the client (approvals, content, third-party access) that could affect the timeline.

## 4. Set Expectations

* How updates will be shared and how often.
* How feedback and revisions will be handled.
* What to do if either side needs to change scope mid-project.

---

# Status / Review Call Structure

## 1. Recap Progress

* Summarize what's been completed since the last update.
* Demo or show working functionality where possible rather than only describing it.

## 2. Surface Issues Early

* Proactively raise any blockers, risks, or scope questions — don't wait to be asked.
* Be specific about impact on timeline if something has shifted.

## 3. Confirm Next Steps

* Confirm what's being worked on next.
* Confirm any decisions or inputs needed from the client before continuing.

---

# General Call Principles

* Let the client talk more than you do, especially in discovery.
* Take notes visibly (or mention you're taking notes) — it signals attentiveness.
* Never promise a timeline or price on the call that you haven't actually thought through; it's fine to say "let me confirm that and follow up in writing."
* Always follow a call with a written summary (see `Closing.md`).

---

# Guiding Principle

The purpose of every call is to leave both sides with more clarity than they had going in — about the problem, the approach, or the next step.
