# Closing

## Purpose

Closing is not a single dramatic moment. It's the natural result of a client feeling understood, seeing a clear path forward, and trusting the person proposing it.

The goal is to make the next step obvious and low-friction, never to pressure a decision.

---

# When to Close

A client is ready to move forward when they:

* Ask about timeline or availability
* Ask about next steps or how to get started
* Confirm scope or budget details
* Stop asking qualifying questions and start asking logistical ones

Recognize these signals and respond by proposing a concrete next step rather than continuing to sell.

---

# How to Close

Always end proposals, calls, and key messages with a clear, specific next action. Examples:

* "If this approach works for you, the next step would be a short call to confirm scope and timeline."
* "I can start once we agree on the first milestone — would [date] work for a kickoff?"
* "I'll put together a short project brief based on this conversation so we're aligned before starting."

Avoid vague endings like "Let me know if you have questions" as the only close — always pair it with a specific suggested action.

---

# Closing After a Call

Within a reasonable time after a discovery call:

* Summarize what was discussed and agreed on.
* Confirm scope, timeline, and next steps in writing.
* Attach or reference a proposal, estimate, or contract if applicable.
* Give a clear call to action (sign, confirm, schedule kickoff).

Written follow-up after a call is not optional — verbal agreement without a written summary creates ambiguity later.

---

# Handling Hesitation at the Close

If the client goes quiet after seeming ready to proceed:

* One clear, low-pressure follow-up is appropriate.
* Ask directly if there are remaining questions or concerns rather than repeating the pitch.
* Accept a "not right now" gracefully — leave the relationship open for the future.

Do not send multiple follow-ups in short succession. It signals desperation rather than confidence.

---

# Closing Fixed-Price Projects

* Confirm scope explicitly in writing before starting.
* Confirm payment structure (milestones, upfront deposit, final payment).
* Get agreement in writing (contract, marketplace agreement, or signed proposal) before beginning work.

---

# Closing Retainer / Ongoing Engagements

* Confirm expected hours or monthly commitment.
* Confirm communication cadence and response times.
* Set a trial period (e.g., first month) if either side wants to validate fit before a longer commitment.

---

# What Not to Do

* Do not create artificial urgency ("I only have one spot left") unless it is genuinely true.
* Do not discount unprompted just to force a decision.
* Do not agree to start without clear scope just to avoid losing the client.
* Do not go silent after the client goes quiet — one graceful follow-up is appropriate.

---

# Guiding Principle

A good close feels like the obvious next step in a conversation built on clarity and trust — not a sales tactic layered on top of it.
