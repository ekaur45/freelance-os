# Review Requests

## Purpose

Reviews and testimonials are one of the highest-leverage assets in freelance work — they directly affect future proposal response and hire rates. Asking well, at the right time, matters as much as the work itself.

---

# Principles

* Ask when satisfaction is highest — right after a successful delivery or milestone, not weeks later.
* Make the ask specific and low-effort; the easier it is to leave a review, the more likely it happens.
* Never ask a client who seemed anything less than fully satisfied — resolve concerns first.
* Match the request to the platform (Upwork review, Fiverr review, LinkedIn recommendation, written testimonial).

---

# Timing

Best moments to ask:

* Immediately after final delivery is confirmed working
* After a client expresses explicit satisfaction ("this looks great," "exactly what we needed")
* At the natural close of a project (see `CompletionMessages.md`)

Avoid asking:

* Before the client has had time to actually use/test the deliverable
* During or right after resolving a problem or complaint
* As the very first message after payment, with no context

---

# Marketplace Review Request (Upwork/Fiverr/Freelancer.com)

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Really glad this turned out well for you. If you have a couple of minutes, a review on [platform] would mean a lot and helps other clients find me for similar work. [Platform-specific link/instruction if applicable.]
>
> No pressure at all — just wanted to ask while it's fresh.

---

# Direct Client Testimonial Request (Outside Marketplaces)

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> I'm glad we got [project] to a great place. Would you be open to sharing a short testimonial about the experience? Even 2-3 sentences on the outcome and what it was like to work together would be really helpful — happy to draft something for you to review and adjust if that's easier.

---

# LinkedIn Recommendation Request

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> If you're open to it, a LinkedIn recommendation would be a great way to help others find me for similar work. Happy to write a draft based on the project for you to edit, if that saves time.

---

# When a Client Doesn't Respond to a Review Request

* One follow-up after a reasonable gap (1-2 weeks) is fine.
* After that, let it go — repeated requests feel transactional and can undo the goodwill from a successful project.

---

# What to Avoid

* Asking before the client has confirmed satisfaction.
* Making the request feel obligatory or tied to future work.
* Offering to write the review yourself and asking the client to just "post it" verbatim without their own input — offering a draft to *edit* is fine, ghostwriting a testimonial they didn't actually approve is not.

---

# Guiding Principle

The best review requests feel like a natural continuation of a good working relationship, not a transaction tacked onto the end of it.
