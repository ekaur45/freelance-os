# Follow-Ups

## Purpose

Follow-ups keep opportunities and relationships moving without becoming pressure or noise. The line between a helpful follow-up and an annoying one is specificity and restraint.

---

# Principles

* Every follow-up should add something new (information, a question, an update) — never just "just checking in" with nothing else.
* Space follow-ups out; two or three well-timed messages beat five rapid ones.
* Make it easy for the client to respond with a single clear question or option.
* Accept silence as an answer after a reasonable number of attempts.

---

# Following Up After a Proposal (No Response)

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Following up on my proposal for [project] in case it got buried. Happy to answer any questions or hop on a quick call if that's easier than text.

Wait a few days before sending; one follow-up is usually enough unless there was a specific reason to expect a response sooner.

---

# Following Up After a Discovery Call

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Great talking today. As discussed, here's a quick summary: [1-2 sentence recap]. I'll [next step] by [date] — let me know if anything needs adjusting before then.

---

# Following Up on a Pending Decision

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Wanted to check in on [proposal/quote] — is there any additional information that would help you decide, or any part of the scope you'd like to revisit?

---

# Following Up on Blocked Work (Waiting on Client)

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Still waiting on [specific item] to continue with [task]. No rush, just flagging it so it doesn't fall off the radar — let me know if you need anything from me to move it along.

---

# Following Up on an Overdue Invoice

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Just a reminder that invoice [#] for [amount] was due on [date]. Let me know if there's anything you need from me to process it, or if there's an issue I should be aware of.

Keep this factual and brief — no need to justify or over-explain the reminder.

---

# When to Stop Following Up

* After 2-3 unanswered follow-ups with no response, stop actively chasing.
* Leave the door open with a final low-pressure message rather than escalating tone.
* Move attention to active opportunities rather than continuing to invest in one that's gone cold.

> No worries if the timing isn't right — feel free to reach out whenever it makes sense. Wishing you the best with the project either way.

---

# Guiding Principle

A good follow-up respects the client's time as much as your own — it should never feel like pressure, only like availability.
