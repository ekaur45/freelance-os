# Greetings & Opening Messages

## Purpose

The first message in any conversation sets the tone for the entire relationship. It should feel personal and specific to the client's project, never like a copy-pasted template.

Refer to `Knowledge/01_Profile/communication-style.md` for overall tone guidance.

---

# Principles

* Reference something specific from the client's post or previous message — never open generically.
* Keep the opening brief; get to relevance quickly.
* Avoid stock phrases like "I hope this message finds you well" or "I am excited to apply."
* Lead with understanding of their problem, not a list of your own skills.

---

# First Message on a Job Post (Proposal Opening)

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> [One sentence showing you understood the specific problem — e.g., "It sounds like your current backend can't keep up with the reporting workload you're describing."]
>
> [One or two sentences connecting relevant experience to that specific problem.]

See `Knowledge/07_Templates/proposal.template.md` for the full proposal structure this opening feeds into.

---

# First Message After Being Contacted Directly

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Thanks for reaching out. [Brief acknowledgment of what they described.]
>
> [A clarifying question or a next step, e.g., "Would it help to jump on a short call to walk through the requirements?"]

---

# Opening a Discovery Call

> "Thanks for making the time. Before I ask questions, is there anything specific you'd like to make sure we cover today?"

---

# Reconnecting After a Gap (Warm Lead, No Recent Contact)

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> It's been a while since we last spoke about [project/topic]. Wanted to check in — is this still something you're looking to move forward on, or has anything changed on your end?

---

# What to Avoid

* Generic enthusiasm ("I'd love to work with you!") without substance behind it.
* Long self-introductions before addressing the client's problem.
* Copy-pasted openers that could apply to any project.

---

# Guiding Principle

Every greeting should make the client think "this person actually read what I wrote" within the first sentence.
