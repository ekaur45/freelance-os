# Completion Messages

## Purpose

How a project's completion is communicated shapes whether the client comes back for more work, leaves a strong review, or refers others. A clear, professional wrap-up matters as much as the delivery itself.

---

# Principles

* Summarize what was delivered clearly, referencing the original goals.
* Provide whatever the client needs to actually use/maintain what was built (access, documentation, credentials).
* Make the transition to next steps (support, future work, review) natural rather than transactional.
* Don't let the relationship go quiet immediately after final payment — a project's end is also a checkpoint for what comes next.

---

# Project/Milestone Completion Message

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> [Project/milestone] is complete. Summary of what was delivered:
>
> - [Deliverable 1]
> - [Deliverable 2]
> - [Deliverable 3]
>
> [Any relevant access details, documentation links, or handoff notes.]
>
> Let me know if anything needs adjusting, or if you'd like to walk through anything together.

---

# Final Project Wrap-Up

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Wanted to formally close out [project name]. Here's a quick recap of what was built and delivered: [brief summary tying back to original goals].
>
> A few things for your records:
> - [Documentation / repository access / credentials]
> - [Any recommendations for next steps or future improvements]
>
> It's been a pleasure working on this with you. If anything comes up — bugs, questions, or new features down the line — feel free to reach out.

---

# Transitioning to Ongoing Support (Upsell Without Being Pushy)

> Now that this is live, I'm happy to help with any ongoing maintenance, monitoring, or new features if that would be useful — either on an as-needed basis or a lighter monthly retainer. No pressure either way; just wanted to mention it's an option if useful down the line.

Refer to `Knowledge/03_Services/maintenance-support.md` for how this service is positioned.

---

# What to Avoid

* Ending abruptly with just "done, let me know if you need anything" — always include a real summary.
* Immediately pitching more work before confirming the client is satisfied with what was delivered.
* Leaving the client without the access/documentation needed to actually use what was built.

---

# Guiding Principle

A completion message should leave the client feeling the project was handled with the same care at the end as it was at the start.
