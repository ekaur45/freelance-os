# Thank You Messages

## Purpose

Genuine appreciation, expressed specifically rather than generically, reinforces relationships and keeps the door open for future work and referrals.

---

# Principles

* Be specific about what you're thanking the client for — generic thanks feel hollow.
* Keep it brief; a thank-you message doesn't need to be long to be sincere.
* Avoid over-the-top language ("This means the world to me!") — stay consistent with a professional, natural tone.

---

# Thank You After Being Hired

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Thanks for choosing me for [project] — looking forward to getting started. I'll follow up shortly with [next step, e.g., a kickoff call time or initial questions].

---

# Thank You After a Review or Testimonial

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Thank you for taking the time to leave that review — I really appreciate it, and it genuinely helps. It was a pleasure working on [project] with you.

---

# Thank You After a Referral

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Thank you for the introduction to [referred contact] — I really appreciate you thinking of me. I'll make sure to represent the referral well.

---

# Thank You After a Difficult Project Goes Well

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Thanks for your patience and clear communication throughout this one — [brief acknowledgment of what made it work, e.g., "the quick feedback on each milestone made a real difference"]. Glad we got it to a great result.

---

# Thank You at the End of a Long-Term Engagement

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Wanted to say thank you for [duration/scope] of working together. It's been a great collaboration, and I've enjoyed [something specific — the problem space, the team, the way feedback was handled]. Wishing you continued success with [product/company], and I'm here if anything comes up down the line.

---

# What to Avoid

* Generic, copy-pasted thank-yous with no specific reference to the client or project.
* Excessive flattery or overly emotional language.
* Using a thank-you message as a disguised pitch for more work — keep gratitude and business asks separate.

---

# Guiding Principle

A thank-you message should make the client feel specifically seen, not just politely acknowledged.
