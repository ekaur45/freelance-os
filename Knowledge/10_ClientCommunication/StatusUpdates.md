# Status Updates

## Purpose

Regular status updates prevent clients from wondering what's happening with their project. Proactive updates build more trust than any amount of reassurance after the fact.

---

# Principles

* Send updates on a predictable cadence (weekly is typical) rather than only when asked.
* Lead with progress, not process — clients care what's done and what's next, not how busy you were.
* Always name the next step, even if the update is otherwise brief.
* Surface risks or blockers in the same update, not in a separate difficult conversation later.

---

# Standard Weekly Update

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Quick update for the week:
>
> **Completed:**
> - [Item 1]
> - [Item 2]
>
> **In progress:**
> - [Item 3]
>
> **Next up:**
> - [Item 4]
>
> [Optional: any blockers or decisions needed from you.]
>
> Let me know if you have any questions.

---

# Milestone Completion Update

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> [Milestone name] is complete. Here's what's included:
>
> - [Feature/deliverable 1]
> - [Feature/deliverable 2]
>
> [Link/demo/access details if applicable.]
>
> Next, I'll be starting on [next milestone]. Let me know if you'd like to review anything before I continue.

---

# Update When Progress Is Slower Than Usual

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Progress this week was slower than planned — [brief, factual reason, e.g., "the third-party API's documentation didn't match its actual behavior, which took extra time to work around"].
>
> Here's where things stand: [brief status]. I expect to be back on track by [date/milestone].

Do not bury a real delay inside an otherwise upbeat update — state it clearly and factually. See `DelayMessages.md` for larger timeline changes.

---

# What to Avoid

* Vague updates ("still working on it") with no specifics.
* Waiting for the client to ask before sharing a status.
* Burying bad news at the end of a long update.
* Over-explaining technical detail the client didn't ask for.

---

# Guiding Principle

A good status update means the client never has to ask "what's the status?" — you've already told them.
