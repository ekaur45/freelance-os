# Delay Messages

## Purpose

Delays happen. How they're communicated matters more than the delay itself. Clients tolerate delays far better when they're told early, honestly, and with a clear revised plan.

---

# Principles

* Communicate a delay as soon as it's known — never wait until the original deadline to mention it's been missed.
* Be factual about the cause without over-apologizing or over-explaining.
* Always include a revised timeline, not just an acknowledgment that things are late.
* Avoid unnecessary apologies (per `Knowledge/01_Profile/communication-style.md`) — one clear acknowledgment is enough; repeated apologizing reads as unprofessional, not considerate.

---

# Proactive Delay Notice (Before Deadline)

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> Wanted to flag this early: [milestone/deliverable] is going to take longer than originally estimated, mainly because of [brief, factual reason].
>
> Revised timeline: [new date].
>
> [If applicable: what this does or doesn't affect elsewhere in the project.]
>
> Let me know if this timeline works on your end or if we need to discuss adjusting scope to hit the original date.

---

# Delay Caused by the Client (Pending Input/Access)

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> [Task] is ready to continue but is currently waiting on [specific input needed — e.g., API credentials, content, a decision on X].
>
> Once that's in hand, I expect [timeframe] to complete it. Let me know if there's anything I can do to help unblock it.

---

# Delay Caused by Unexpected Technical Complexity

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> While working on [feature], I ran into [specific issue] that wasn't apparent from the original requirements. Resolving it properly will take an additional [timeframe].
>
> I'd rather take the time to do this correctly than rush a fix that causes problems later. New expected completion: [date].

---

# When a Delay Affects Budget (Fixed-Price Projects)

> Hi [Client Name],
>
> The additional work needed for [reason] goes beyond the original scope we agreed on. I want to flag this before continuing rather than after.
>
> Options:
> 1. [Adjust scope to stay within original budget/timeline]
> 2. [Extend budget/timeline to include the additional work]
>
> Happy to discuss which makes more sense for you.

---

# What to Avoid

* Silence until asked — this is the single biggest trust-breaker.
* Excessive apologizing that reads as anxious rather than professional.
* Vague timelines ("soon," "shortly") instead of specific dates.
* Blaming third parties or circumstances at length instead of focusing on the plan forward.

---

# Guiding Principle

A well-communicated delay rarely damages trust. An undisclosed one almost always does.
